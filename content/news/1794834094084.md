<section class='story' id='story-1'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://securityaffairs.com/203085/cyber-crime/german-military-grounds-quantum-systems-drones-following-reported-cyberattack.html'>German Military Grounds Quantum Systems Drones Following Reported <span class='story__last'>Cyberattack</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--supply-chain' title='Compromise of a vendor, package, dependency or CI pipeline.'>Supply chain</span> <span class='story__source'>securityaffairs.com</span></p>
<p>German defense firm Quantum Systems confirmed an active cyberattack at the end of October 2026, prompting the German military to ground its Quantum Systems drones according to reports. Citing internal sources, Der Spiegel and the Financial Times reported that attackers may have distributed a malicious update file for QBase, the Windows ground-control and mission-planning software used for the Vector reconnaissance drone known as Falke, pointing to a potential supply chain compromise of its update server. Quantum Systems stated its security teams contained the threat in collaboration with authorities and that operating its unmanned systems poses no risk to customers or operators.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-2'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/tsmc-employee-data-breach-linked-to-cloud-provider/'>TSMC employee data breach linked to cloud <span class='story__last'>provider</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--breach' title='Data stolen, leaked or exposed.'>Data breach</span> <span class='story__source'>bleepingcomputer.com</span></p>
<p>Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) confirmed that personal employee data was leaked online in a breach linked to a cloud provider. The disclosure followed a threat actor putting 6.9GB of information allegedly stolen from the chipmaker up for auction.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-3'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/linux/new-noisytcp-linux-kernel-flaw-lets-attackers-crash-servers/'>New NoisyTCP vulnerability lets attackers crash Linux <span class='story__last'>servers</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--vulnerability' title='A security flaw, its disclosure or its patch.'>Vulnerability</span> <span class='story__source'>bleepingcomputer.com</span></p>
<p>Security researchers disclosed an 11-year-old Linux kernel vulnerability dubbed &#x27;NoisyTCP&#x27; that affects nearly all Linux distributions. The flaw enables attackers to crash Linux servers running Nginx, Apache, and SSH services under default configurations.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-4'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/bo-team-exploits-log4shell-in-vmware.html'>BO Team Exploits Log4Shell in VMware vCenter, Compromises Russian Firm&#x27;s Code-Signing <span class='story__last'>Server</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--exploited' title='Exploited in the wild or zero-day: attackers already use it.'>Exploited</span> <span class='nb nb--supply-chain' title='Compromise of a vendor, package, dependency or CI pipeline.'>Supply chain</span> <span class='nb nb--apt' title='Nation-state or advanced persistent threat actor.'>APT</span> <span class='story__source'>thehackernews.com</span></p>
<p>Kaspersky&#x27;s ICS CERT team documented a months-long intrusion at a Russian software developer after the firm sought assistance in June 2025. Investigators attributed the attack to the threat actor BO Team, also tracked as Black Owl and Hoody Hyena, which exploited the Log4Shell vulnerability in VMware vCenter to compromise the developer&#x27;s infrastructure and software code-signing server.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-5'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://securityaffairs.com/203188/ai/artex-how-cybercriminals-use-ai-agents-to-scale-their-attacks.html'>ARTEX: How Cybercriminals Use AI Agents to Scale Their <span class='story__last'>Attacks</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--apt' title='Nation-state or advanced persistent threat actor.'>APT</span> <span class='story__source'>securityaffairs.com</span></p>
<p>Between 3 October and 5 November, Amazon&#x27;s MadPot decoy network captured campaign records from AI agents that Amazon Threat Intelligence assessed with moderate confidence were running on the open-source pentesting framework ARTEX and operated by China-linked groups. The agents targeted exposed AI gateways and development platforms such as LiteLLM, One API, New API, Veloera, Sub2API, and Dify to steal AI provider API keys, which were then fed back into the attackers&#x27; own model gateway to self-fund further credential theft and test deepfake ID bypasses. Amazon notified potentially affected AWS customers after reviewing the captured agent records.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-6'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://securityaffairs.com/203099/apt/silentair-and-liminal-panda-target-telecom-networks-to-spy-on-subscribers.html'>SilentAir and LIMINAL PANDA Target Telecom Networks to Spy on <span class='story__last'>Subscribers</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--apt' title='Nation-state or advanced persistent threat actor.'>APT</span> <span class='nb nb--malware' title='Malware family, campaign or tooling.'>Malware</span> <span class='story__source'>securityaffairs.com</span></p>
<p>Telecom security firm Enea published research detailing mobile network espionage by the newly identified group &#x27;SilentAir&#x27;, active since before 2025, alongside ongoing 2026 intrusions by China-linked LIMINAL PANDA. Enea&#x27;s firewall detected and blocked the Unix-based GTPDoor malware exploiting the GTP-C signalling protocol at several operators starting in the first quarter of 2026. The company directly observed or strongly suspects 5 to 10 infected mobile operators and estimates several dozen may be compromised worldwide across Asia, Africa, and Latin America, noting that links between SilentAir and LIMINAL PANDA remain unconfirmed.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-7'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/sansar-linux-cryptomining-malware-uses.html'>SANSAR Linux Cryptomining Malware Uses Modified NVIDIA Library to Hide GPU <span class='story__last'>Activity</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--malware' title='Malware family, campaign or tooling.'>Malware</span> <span class='story__source'>thehackernews.com</span></p>
<p>A financially motivated threat cluster tracked as REF3364 is conducting a Linux cryptocurrency mining campaign using a Rust-based orchestrator called &#x27;SANSAR&#x27;. The toolkit receives commands over the TOR anonymity network to manage CPU and GPU miners while using a modified NVIDIA library to tamper with readings and conceal high GPU workloads.</p>
</section>
