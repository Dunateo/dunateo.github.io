<section class='story' id='story-1'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://securityaffairs.com/203062/cyber-crime/extortion-group-claims-theft-of-nearly-one-billion-expedia-traveler-records.html'>Extortion Group Claims Theft of Nearly One Billion Expedia Traveler <span class='story__last'>Records</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--breach' title='Data stolen, leaked or exposed.'>Data breach</span> <span class='nb nb--supply-chain' title='Compromise of a vendor, package, dependency or CI pipeline.'>Supply chain</span> <span class='story__source'>securityaffairs.com</span></p>
<p>Expedia Group confirmed that attackers breached its systems through a compromised third-party data platform provider in a supply chain attack affecting multiple companies, which cybersecurity firms Wiz and Sentra linked to a stolen vendor key from Google Cloud Platform partner Qubole. The newly formed Russian extortion group &#x27;n0n&#x27; claimed responsibility for stealing data on nearly one billion travelers, including more than 830 million loyalty program records, 333 million consumer account records, and 58 million traveler profiles, as well as source code. Expedia has not confirmed the group&#x27;s figures, stated that its investigation is ongoing, and noted that its platforms remain safe for travelers and business partners.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-2'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://securityaffairs.com/203126/data-breach/data-breach-at-la-poste-subsidiary-maileva-affects-22-million-people-in-france.html'>Data Breach at La Poste Subsidiary Maileva Affects 22 Million People in <span class='story__last'>France</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--breach' title='Data stolen, leaked or exposed.'>Data breach</span> <span class='story__source'>securityaffairs.com</span></p>
<p>A mid-September cyberattack on La Poste subsidiary Maileva exposed the first names, last names, and postal addresses of 22 million people in France who received documents sent through its platform, though bank details were not compromised. Affected organizations include social housing providers, Inovie group medical laboratories, and Foch Hospital in Suresnes via subcontractor Kiwee.care, where a hacker reportedly read payment reminders containing invoice numbers, treatment dates, and amounts owed. Maileva notified affected companies and France&#x27;s data protection authority, the CNIL, and filed a criminal complaint as a judicial investigation proceeds.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-3'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/stolen-fireworks-ai-key-led-to-cursor.html'>Cursor Says Stolen Fireworks AI Key Led to Trojanized Claude Code Extension <span class='story__last'>Incident</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--supply-chain' title='Compromise of a vendor, package, dependency or CI pipeline.'>Supply chain</span> <span class='nb nb--breach' title='Data stolen, leaked or exposed.'>Data breach</span> <span class='nb nb--malware' title='Malware family, campaign or tooling.'>Malware</span> <span class='story__source'>thehackernews.com</span></p>
<p>Cursor disclosed that the compromise of Anthropic&#x27;s official Claude Code extension on its extension marketplace resulted from a stolen access key exposed during a breach targeting Fireworks AI. In a Friday update, Cursor stated that an attacker obtained the access key issued to Fireworks AI and used it to copy data, leading to the trojanized extension incident.</p>
</section>
<section class='story' id='story-4'>
<h2 class='story__title'><a href='https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/italy-fines-meta-805-million-over.html'>Italy Fines Meta €805 Million Over Facebook&#x27;s Android Gallery Access Without Explicit <span class='story__last'>Consent</span></a></h2>
<p class='story__tags'><span class='nb nb--law' title='Arrests, takedowns, sanctions, regulation or government policy.'>Law &amp; policy</span> <span class='story__source'>thehackernews.com</span></p>
<p>Italy&#x27;s data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta €805 million over a Facebook for Android feature that suggests photos and videos from users&#x27; phone galleries for them to post. The regulator found that the feature was enabled by default and accessed Italian users&#x27; photos, videos, and related metadata without their explicit consent.</p>
</section>
