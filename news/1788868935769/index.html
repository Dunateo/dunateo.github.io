CyberNews: 08/09/2026 Edition

Published by Dunateo on 2026-09-08

Today’s roundup

  • Zero-Day Vulnerability in Popular VPN Software Exploited in the Wild
  • Major Data Breach Hits Healthcare Provider, Exposing Millions of Patient Records
  • Ransomware Group Targets Municipalities with New LockBit 3.0 Variant

    • Summary

    A critical zero-day vulnerability (CVE-2023-XXXX) has been identified in a widely used VPN software, impacting versions 5.x and 6.x. This flaw allows unauthenticated remote code execution and is being actively exploited in the wild by state-sponsored actors to gain initial access to corporate networks. The vulnerability was discovered after unusual network activity was detected at several government agencies and critical infrastructure organizations. Patches are currently unavailable, and vendors recommend immediate mitigation measures, including disabling vulnerable services or applying temporary workarounds such as IP whitelisting and multi-factor authentication for VPN access.

    HealthCare Global, a prominent healthcare provider, has suffered a significant data breach resulting in the exposure of an estimated 5 million patient records. The compromised data includes sensitive personal identifiable information (PII) such as names, addresses, dates of birth, social security numbers, and medical insurance details. The incident was caused by a misconfigured cloud storage bucket that was left publicly accessible. Independent security researchers discovered the vulnerability and notified HealthCare Global. Investigations are ongoing, with potential financial losses estimated in the tens of millions due from regulatory fines and remediation costs.

    A new variant of the LockBit 3.0 ransomware, featuring enhanced encryption algorithms and improved evasion techniques, has been observed targeting municipal government organizations across North America. The ransomware group, known for its double-extortion tactics, is exploiting unpatched vulnerabilities in remote desktop protocols (RDP) and leveraging phishing attacks to achieve initial access. Several cities have reported disruptions to their IT systems, impacting public services, and negotiations with the attackers are reportedly underway in some cases. Experts recommend immediate patching of RDP vulnerabilities, strong network segmentation, and robust backup strategies.
