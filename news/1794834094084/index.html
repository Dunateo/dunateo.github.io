German Military Grounds Quantum Systems Drones Following Reported Cyberattack

Supply chain securityaffairs.com

German defense firm Quantum Systems confirmed an active cyberattack at the end of October 2026, prompting the German military to ground its Quantum Systems drones according to reports. Citing internal sources, Der Spiegel and the Financial Times reported that attackers may have distributed a malicious update file for QBase, the Windows ground-control and mission-planning software used for the Vector reconnaissance drone known as Falke, pointing to a potential supply chain compromise of its update server. Quantum Systems stated its security teams contained the threat in collaboration with authorities and that operating its unmanned systems poses no risk to customers or operators.

TSMC employee data breach linked to cloud provider

Data breach bleepingcomputer.com

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) confirmed that personal employee data was leaked online in a breach linked to a cloud provider. The disclosure followed a threat actor putting 6.9GB of information allegedly stolen from the chipmaker up for auction.

New NoisyTCP vulnerability lets attackers crash Linux servers

Vulnerability bleepingcomputer.com

Security researchers disclosed an 11-year-old Linux kernel vulnerability dubbed 'NoisyTCP' that affects nearly all Linux distributions. The flaw enables attackers to crash Linux servers running Nginx, Apache, and SSH services under default configurations.

BO Team Exploits Log4Shell in VMware vCenter, Compromises Russian Firm's Code-Signing Server

Exploited Supply chain APT thehackernews.com

Kaspersky's ICS CERT team documented a months-long intrusion at a Russian software developer after the firm sought assistance in June 2025. Investigators attributed the attack to the threat actor BO Team, also tracked as Black Owl and Hoody Hyena, which exploited the Log4Shell vulnerability in VMware vCenter to compromise the developer's infrastructure and software code-signing server.

ARTEX: How Cybercriminals Use AI Agents to Scale Their Attacks

APT securityaffairs.com

Between 3 October and 5 November, Amazon's MadPot decoy network captured campaign records from AI agents that Amazon Threat Intelligence assessed with moderate confidence were running on the open-source pentesting framework ARTEX and operated by China-linked groups. The agents targeted exposed AI gateways and development platforms such as LiteLLM, One API, New API, Veloera, Sub2API, and Dify to steal AI provider API keys, which were then fed back into the attackers' own model gateway to self-fund further credential theft and test deepfake ID bypasses. Amazon notified potentially affected AWS customers after reviewing the captured agent records.

SilentAir and LIMINAL PANDA Target Telecom Networks to Spy on Subscribers

APT Malware securityaffairs.com

Telecom security firm Enea published research detailing mobile network espionage by the newly identified group 'SilentAir', active since before 2025, alongside ongoing 2026 intrusions by China-linked LIMINAL PANDA. Enea's firewall detected and blocked the Unix-based GTPDoor malware exploiting the GTP-C signalling protocol at several operators starting in the first quarter of 2026. The company directly observed or strongly suspects 5 to 10 infected mobile operators and estimates several dozen may be compromised worldwide across Asia, Africa, and Latin America, noting that links between SilentAir and LIMINAL PANDA remain unconfirmed.

SANSAR Linux Cryptomining Malware Uses Modified NVIDIA Library to Hide GPU Activity

Malware thehackernews.com

A financially motivated threat cluster tracked as REF3364 is conducting a Linux cryptocurrency mining campaign using a Rust-based orchestrator called 'SANSAR'. The toolkit receives commands over the TOR anonymity network to manage CPU and GPU miners while using a modified NVIDIA library to tamper with readings and conceal high GPU workloads.
