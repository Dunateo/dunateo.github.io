Bell Canada says 'agentic' hacker stole enterprise customer data

bleepingcomputer.com

Bell Canada has confirmed that an 'agentic' threat actor breached its systems on November 17, 2026, and stole enterprise customer data. The stolen information includes data from a system used to order and provision telecommunications circuits.

Attackers' AI Agents Found a Zero-Day in Medical-Imaging Software and Gained Root Access

Exploited APT Vulnerability thehackernews.com

Suspected China-linked hackers used autonomous AI agents to find a previously unknown flaw in MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging server, and to gain root access to a university server, Amazon Threat Intelligence said this week. The flaw allows an attacker with no account to write files outside the server's storage folder and affects MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier in the default setup.

Hackers Exploit Critical VMware, Cisco, and Apache Flaws as Espionage Attacks Emerge

Exploited Vulnerability thehackernews.com

  • CVE-2026-129448 Critical

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency on Friday added four flaws affecting Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway, SAP NetWeaver and Broadcom VMware vCenter to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing evidence of active exploitation. One of the listed flaws, CVE-2026-129448 with a CVSS score of 9.8, is a deserialization of untrusted data vulnerability.

JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard Report Third-Party Data Breaches After N0n Claims

Data breach Supply chain securityaffairs.com

JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard confirmed incidents involving external vendors after the 'N0n' extortion group claimed to have stolen customer information and published data allegedly taken from their systems. JPMorgan said attackers may have accessed marketing data held by its service providers, including customer names and email addresses, while Visa said the incident involved a third-party provider in the Asia-Pacific region and Mastercard said a limited number of email addresses of cardholders in a specific benefits program were exposed. The group listed the three companies on its leak site on November 16, and a Reuters review found JPMorgan-related files containing more than 16,000 customer email addresses.

GnuPG Warns Critical GPGME Flaw Could Expose App Data via Malicious OpenPGP Keys

Vulnerability thehackernews.com

  • CVE-2026-129975 TBD

The GnuPG project warned that a malicious OpenPGP key with an unusually long user ID can trigger a critical memory bug in GPGME, the standard library programs use to work with GnuPG, and that the flaw could be used to steal data from affected applications. The bug, tracked as CVE-2026-129975, affects all software that uses GPGME before version 2.3.0, and fixed versions have been released.

CVE-2026-23871: WhatsApp Bug Helped Scammers Make Messages Look Legitimate

Exploited Vulnerability securityaffairs.com

  • CVE-2026-23871 Medium
  • CVE-2026-102769 Medium
  • CVE-2026-44908 High

WhatsApp patched three security flaws, including CVE-2026-23871, a case of incomplete validation of external ad-reply URLs in WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android before 2.26.17.4 that could cause a target's device to reveal its IP information to an attacker. WhatsApp said it found the issue while disrupting a scam campaign and believes a scammer was using it to make scam messages seem more credible. The other fixes address a SQL injection, CVE-2026-102769, in WhatsApp for Android before 2.26.35.74, and CVE-2026-44908, in the remote-attestation verifier that confirms trust with Meta AI's confidential computing backend.

Bazuka Android Malware Uses Fake Screen Overlays to Hide Credential Theft and Device Control

Malware thehackernews.com

Dutch mobile security company ThreatFabric disclosed a previously unknown Android malware framework called Bazuka that offers large language model integration for victim management and control at scale. The framework uses fake screen overlays to hide credential theft and device control, and was discovered in late October 2026 during an investigation into an NFC-relay banking Trojan.

A Single Malicious Webpage Let Researchers Hijack Meta Muse and Access Private Chats

Vulnerability thehackernews.com

Researchers showed that a malicious webpage could extract a Meta Muse user's private conversations and stored memories from the assistant without triggering any approval prompt, and that the same attack could be extended to reach the credentials Muse stores for a user's connected accounts. The work was a laboratory demonstration, not an attack on real users.

CTO of ‘RansomwareHelp’ Indicted for Wire Fraud

Law & policy Ransomware krebsonsecurity.com

The chief technology officer and co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com, 52-year-old Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar of Miami and Medellin, Colombia, has been charged with wire fraud after an FBI sting operation found the Florida company was paying attackers for decryption keys and charging victims a markup. According to an indictment returned this week in Rhode Island, Palacio requested the lowest possible price for a decryptor from a fictitious ransomware group run by the FBI and then charged the fake victim firm $16,010, including an initial $2,750 diagnostic fee. The now-defunct website had urged victims not to pay the ransom, claiming its proprietary cryptographic tools could restore 100 percent of data.
